HOLY ORDER — THE FIRST DISPLACEMENT
Original desert mission / The village update

Double-click click2play.desktop and choose DESERT, then BEGIN and READY.
The same menu includes the final Winter and Omaha missions. Esc > Mission Menu
returns to mission selection. Only one mission runs at a time.

Version 0.7 defaults to Low graphics in every mission. F6 cycles Low / Standard /
High, and F10 toggles the live FPS counter. The knight keeps 300 maximum health.
Omaha now has two 600-person American waves and three engineers per boat.
See OMAHA-START-HERE.txt for the updated landing and DOUBLE-WAVE-UPDATE.txt for performance details.
Grenades make localized breaches; WEAPON-RATES.txt documents firearm timing.
See DOUBLE-WAVE-UPDATE.txt for the latest changes and PERFORMANCE-UPDATE.txt for the prior shared graphics update.

WHAT CHANGED
The encounter now occupies an abandoned, damaged frontier village with seven
enterable houses, courtyards, a well, doors, roof ramps, and climbable cover.
The lone sentry remains well outside the five-man village position.
Inside: two assault soldiers, two rifle defenders and one Breda M37 gunner.

A quiet sentry takedown can preserve surprise. Rifle reports carry across the
encounter. Hearing one provides a direction and an uncertain search area;
aimed shots still require a clear view. Assault soldiers investigate and
alternate advancing with covering fire. Defenders seek cover and can dig a
shallow scrape when the threat seems distant. Nearby footsteps, shots, and
near misses interrupt that work and provoke cover or repositioning. Navigation
updates when doors open or geometry changes. Friendly soldiers use the same
perception and tactical controller, with the opposing side as their targets.
The British patrol appears after the fighting, so its tactics are primarily
infrastructure for future encounters, rather than a new allied battle here.

The M37 has a loaded 20-round tray and precisely three spare trays in its box.
Reloading consumes a tray. Once it is empty, its gunner uses his Carcano.
F lets you operate an abandoned usable gun; your shots consume the same supply.
Its 8 mm ammunition is separate from the Carcano's 6.5 mm ammunition.

Each Italian begins with six loaded rifle rounds and three six-round reserve
clips. Shooting and reloading deplete those inventories. Bodies yield their
remaining ammunition, pocket lint and an entrenching tool. If already armed,
you also strip any loaded rounds from the corpse rifle. Otherwise, recovering
a replacement rifle preserves the rounds still inside it. Lint stacks without
a current use. Rifles can be broken by hits; another corpse can supply a
replacement. Empty soldiers use knives. Your sword and shovel can also break;
your armoured fists remain available.

Walls, doors, roofs, timber, sandbags, props, rocks and the battlefield ground
accept persistent local damage. Openings alter collision as well as appearance.
Wood yields more readily than masonry or rock. The shovel [4] excavates soil;
grenades and demolition charges make larger breaches and craters. The solid
ground is 24 metres deep inside the bounded encounter. This is a prototype
of local geometric destruction, not a structural-engineering simulation:
unsupported pieces do not yet undergo a general physics collapse. Atmospheric
particles, sky and objective markers are not physical objects to demolish.

OPTIONAL SUPPLIES
Three Breda M35 grenades are in the eastern village store.
Three preassembled demolition satchels are in the rear western engineer's store.
Carry one satchel at a time. Equip it with 5, look at nearby geometry and left
click to place it, or right click to lob it. B detonates the wired charge.
F at a deployed satchel recovers it. There is no automatic timer on the satchel.
The cable pays out as you move; beyond the game's 30-metre allowance it breaks,
leaving an inert charge that you can recover. No unlimited or wireless charges.
Explosives are entirely optional: the rifle, sword and fists can finish the test.

THE FIRST TEST
Start behind a large rock with sword and armour. Sneak, crawl or charge the
isolated sentry, recover his rifle, and choose your approach to the village.
The short pickup scene and the knight's bolt-action-rifle joke are subtitled.
If the sentry fired, the recovered rifle contains fewer than six loaded rounds.
Search bodies with F for what remains in their pockets.
Clear the other five soldiers. The last death brings a stopped British light
truck to the nearby road. Follow the gold marker and approach the patrol to
climb aboard and receive a highly questionable cup of tea. That subtitled
scene ends at the test-complete screen. J displays the objective sequence.

VISUAL PASS
The original procedural Carcano has a contoured walnut stock, slimmer steel
parts, an open rear V-notch and thin front blade. The eye now sits farther
behind the sights, reducing the oversized rear-sight silhouette. Settled sights
share the shot's aim point, including when crouched, prone or leaning.
Aiming eases in, reduces mouse sensitivity and walking bob, and magnifies the
view. The bolt cycles visibly; reloading shows a six-round en-bloc clip.
Rounded prop and armour geometry, plaster wear and irregular rocks,
warm sunlight, cooler shadows, ACES tone mapping and light dust haze establish
the first cinematic pass. Characters and much of the environment remain
simplified original prototype art; this is not MW2/Battlefield-level production.

HISTORY / FICTION / GAME RULES
The order mistook a time-displacement machine for the Ark of the Covenant.
Its members were scattered across centuries. The knight's plate equipment is
inspired by around 1450; his order and this particular village fight are fictional.
The setting is the Capuzzo frontier, Libya, 14 June 1940, during the opening
British–Italian fighting. The patrol truck is Morris CS8-inspired, not a Jeep.

The Carcano M91 uses six-round en-bloc clips, not five-round stripper clips.
The Breda M37 uses 20-round rigid feed trays, not detachable box magazines.
The demolition satchel is a period-style field-engineer prop; an exact Italian
issued model for this June 1940 encounter has not been established. Its one-item
carry limit, 30-metre cable and simplified operation are explicit game rules,
not historical equipment limits. There are no real construction instructions.
The M35's simplified impact logic includes a game-only timed failsafe.
Armour mitigates melee blows, but does not make rifle fire harmless. Health,
weapon durability, one-hit sword/rifle takedowns and fast charges are balancing
choices. Reloading conserves rounds but abstracts detailed clip handling.
