HOLY ORDER 0.8 — OMAHA TOWN AND ARMOR

PLAY
Open click2play.desktop > OMAHA. Deploy plays the approach; SKIP TO RAMP
jumps to just before arrival. Enter also skips during the approach.
The cinematic ends before the ramp lowers. Wait for the file to clear,
then use WASD to walk off yourself. F does not teleport you ashore.
Boats return after unloading, with a 36-second deadline for the player's craft.
Staying aboard until it returns to sea ends the landing attempt.
Wave two lands two minutes after wave one (2:32 from a normal deployment).
Sprint and melee charges no longer consume stamina in Winter, Desert or Omaha.

FORTIFIED TOWN
The existing 100 beach defenders are followed by 150 additional town Germans.
24 houses have ground floors, upper floors and attics, working collision floors
and stairwells. There are 96 indoor stations and 54 street positions, including
42 additional mounted MG42 positions. Window apertures, sandbags, barricades,
paved streets, kerbs, shutters, chimneys and slate/terracotta roofs replace the
old grassy-looking house surfaces. Town infantry stays inactive until contact
or the inland approach, then fights using the normal visibility and ammo rules.
The full beach force is still 1,200 Americans in two waves of 600.

PANZER IVs
Three tanks patrol connected streets between the front and rear of town.
Their targets must be detected and visible; the player gets no priority.
They can pursue a last seen contact and reposition at crossroads. They remain
inside the town boundaries and cannot drive down onto the beach.
Each has a rotating turret, a long 75 mm-style cannon, coaxial MG, track runs,
road wheels and side skirts. Ammunition is finite: 32 shells and 1,600 MG rounds.
Small arms cannot drain hull health. Hundreds of concentrated track hits can
immobilize a tank. Several close grenade blasts can also break tracks, but
hand grenades cannot destroy the hull. Broken tracks do not stop its turret.
Bazooka impacts damage the hull; side/rear hits are more effective than frontal
hits, and several penetrating hits are normally needed to destroy a tank.
These are gameplay damage values, not an engineering armor-penetration model.

AI-ONLY BAZOOKAS
Eight launchers: four per wave, each with six total rockets. Carriers retain
ordinary rifles. Each launcher owns exactly one carrier and at most one loader.
A loader must be alive and within 2.8 metres to complete a 6.8-second reload.
The buddy follows alongside, uses a rifle, and matches the carrier's cover stance.
If a loader dies, another eligible rifleman is nominated. If the carrier dies,
the launcher and remaining rockets stay at the drop point; an eligible friendly
can recover it, then receive a different loader. Claims prevent duplicate crews
or one helper serving several launchers. Engineers retain their wire assignment until the wire belts have been crossed.
The loader can spot a tank, and the carrier seeks a clear firing position if
nearby cover or friendly troops obstruct the shot or backblast.
Teams require a clear line of fire and backblast space. The player cannot pick
up or use bazookas. Protect the teams and open routes for them into town.

WEAPONS AND HANDS
The eleven WWII firearm models have separate dimensions, furniture, magazines,
belt boxes, shrouds, muzzle shapes and sight positions. Walnut, steel and painted
parts have separate finishes. Garand clips, Mauser stripper clips, detachable
magazines and belts have corresponding reload props and motions.
Existing caliber compatibility, partial magazines, carry limits, finite supplies
and firing-rate clocks are preserved. The knight's gauntlets wrap around the gun
and forearms extend toward the shoulders; sights align for every firearm.
Desert retains its separate Carcano and Breda models, with the revised rifle hands.

DESTRUCTION
Repeated bullets can punch small local openings in vulnerable wood, sandbags
and town masonry. Grenades damage walls and leave shallow ground scars. Cannon
shells and rockets cause stronger local structural damage and larger craters.
Remaining wall pieces still block movement and bullets. Reinforced beach bunkers
remain resistant to small arms and hand grenades.
A house checks remaining foundation support capacity and the distribution of
supports around its centre. Losing sufficient support collapses its shell and
kills occupants, including those upstairs. Collapses leave eight solid rubble
pieces instead of hundreds of continually simulated fragments.
Damage edits are queued, coalesced and bounded; they are not unlimited per-bullet
CSG cuts. The environment and original soldier art remain stylized prototypes,
not a photorealistic or surveyed 1:1 reconstruction of Normandy.

PERFORMANCE AND VALIDATION
Low / Max FPS remains the default, with 40% 3D resolution in Omaha, 20 Hz
interpolated simulation, spatial AI queries, instanced distant troops, bounded
projectiles/effects and persistent instanced bodies. Batched scenery releases
its redundant individual visual nodes and recreates only panels being damaged.
No software or art packages were downloaded or installed for this update.
Headless tests exercise game logic, collision, navigation and ammunition; they
do not measure the laptop GPU or verify the final rendered appearance. Read F10's
FPS counter during actual combat. The expanded battle adds simulation work.
Detailed current test results and CPU timings are in readiness-report.json and
reviews/omaha/town-armor-checks.json.
