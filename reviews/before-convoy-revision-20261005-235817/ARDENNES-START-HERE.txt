HOLY ORDER — ROAD OF IRON
Winter convoy mission, v0.3.0

START
Double-click click2play.desktop in this folder. Choose DEPLOY at the title screen.
click2play-desert.desktop opens the original First Contact mission. Both title
screens also have a button for the other mission. F11 toggles fullscreen.

THE ENCOUNTER
A fictional Ardennes road encounter during the winter of 1944–45. Nine GMC CCKW
style trucks carry nine planned twelve-person squad loads: 107 American AI
soldiers and you. Twelve is this convoy's chosen squad load, NOT a claim about
maximum truck seating. If only the two end trucks are lost, the seven central
loads contain 83 other soldiers and you. Random casualties can reduce that.

There are 80 Germans in eight defended positions. Two staged Panzerfaust shots
are authored to hit the lead and rear trucks. The two performers stay concealed
until their cues; once revealed, they are ordinary killable soldiers. Those two
projectiles alone have guaranteed impacts. Other shots, grenades, rockets,
vehicle losses and casualties use the live simulation. You can die in transit.
An early random truck loss can bring the authored strikes forward.

Survivors dismount. The force clears ROADBLOCK, FIR RIDGE, then UPPER DUGOUTS.
Each area has prepared ground, an MG position, sandbags, timber shelters and
communication trenches. The surrounding map has hills, forest, rocks and
artillery craters. Killing the last German ends this test mission.

You remain the displaced armored knight, carrying a sword, shovel, gauntlets,
four Mk 2 grenades and an M1918A2 BAR: 20 loaded rounds and 120 spare rounds.
There is no voice acting. Orders and the knight's remarks use subtitles.

BODIES, EQUIPMENT AND INVENTORY
Bodies, detached body parts and dropped guns remain for the mission. Further
bullet hits and explosions can damage and detach body parts. A death drops the
weapon with its actual remaining loaded ammunition. A broken gun can still be
picked up but cannot fire. You carry one firearm; swapping drops the old one.
Mounted M2 Brownings remain on their mounts and are not handheld pickups.
MG 34/42 pickups use a portable 50-round load; excess belt ammunition stays on
the ground. Carried weapon condition and loaded rounds survive a swap.

Grenades share a hard four-item limit. Looted German M24 grenades retain their
visual type. Excess grenades stay on the body. Bodies transfer remaining stores,
not a fresh preset loot reward. An enemy who used ammunition has less to take.

Spare ammunition shares a pack with 24 space units and a 7.5 kg ammunition limit.
This is a scenario belt-and-pack allowance, not a universal historical maximum.
Loaded ammunition is on the weapon, separate from spare pack space. Containers
are tracked individually; a partly full magazine still occupies its full space.

Feed                              Pack space each     Additional type limit
20-round BAR magazine             3.0                 160 spare rounds
8-round M1 en-bloc clip            1.0                 128 spare rounds
5-round Mauser stripper clip       0.5                 120 spare rounds
10-round G43 magazine              2.0                 120 spare rounds
32-round MP40 magazine             3.0                 192 spare rounds
50-round 7.92 mm MG belt            6.0                 200 spare rounds
100-round .50 belt                15.0                 also limited by weight

Space and weight limits apply across all calibers together. Different calibers
never convert into one another. Repacking compatible cartridges from another
feed takes an extra reload delay; detailed individual cartridge handling is
abstracted. Empty containers are discarded. Tab lists the actual containers.
DROP SPARE AMMO PACK places that ammunition on the ground; F recovers what fits.
Uncollected ammunition is retained on its body or dropped bundle.

AI AND DESTRUCTION
A convoy commander, three maneuver-group leaders and individual squad leaders
assign and execute area plans. Living subordinates take over after deaths. The
Americans can advance and fight without player participation. See
ARDENNES-TACTICS.txt and scripts/ardennes/tactical_plans.json for the reusable
plans and current implementation. This is a fictional reinforced detachment,
not a complete reproduction of a historical company's table of organization.

Hold orders keep defenders inside their assigned perimeters. Cautious assault
orders still advance, with slower bounds. Squads take cover, react to nearby
misses, dig shallow cover, throw finite grenades and fall back to personal
weapons or close combat. Both teams check firing dispersion and nearby allies
before firing, and reject grenade targets too close to friendlies. Friendly
fire remains lethal. Avoidance is not an immunity rule.

Trees, timber, sandbags, rocks, vehicle parts and equipment can be damaged.
Wood fails sooner than rock. Damaged surfaces get physical cuts; worn-out
pieces collapse. Terrain uses bounded heightfield deformation for dependable
trench and crater collision. Walls and props retain cut-through geometry.

CURRENT PROTOTYPE LIMITS
The models, movement and effects are original procedural prototype assets,
not scanned uniforms or AAA animation. The historical weapon names, basic
silhouettes, calibers and feeds are represented; small fittings and reload
animations are simplified. Armor and penetration are game approximations.
There is no voice acting, multiplayer, save system or campaign persistence.

Destruction is finite and budgeted. Soil supports pits and trenches, not tunnels
or overhang excavation. Unsupported debris does not have a complete structural
engineering simulation. Individual pine needles are visual geometry. Damaged
cover can leave rubble; corpse parts have simple physical shapes. AI navigation
uses a coarse terrain grid and may hesitate in unusually complex rubble.
The ambush is deliberately dangerous; casualty balance needs player testing.

PERFORMANCE CHECK
The local RTX 3070 Laptop test rendered the live convoy and battle at 1440x900
with 4x MSAA: 16.7 ms median frame time (about 60 FPS), 20.7 ms at the 95th
percentile, and 41.1 ms at the 99th percentile. Some heavier destruction still
causes brief hitches. The automated observer does not measure every possible
player viewpoint. Screenshot capture overhead is included in the timing log.
See reviews/ardennes-performance.json for the recorded run.
