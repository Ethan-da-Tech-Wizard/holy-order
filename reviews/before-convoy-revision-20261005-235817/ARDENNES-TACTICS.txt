HOLY ORDER / REUSABLE WINTER ENCOUNTER PLANS

This document describes the game's authored AI, not a real-world field manual.

AREA ORDER
1. ROADBLOCK: positions 0–2, the convoy ambush and its nearby defenses.
2. FIR RIDGE: positions 3–5, higher forest approaches and sheltered gun sites.
3. UPPER DUGOUTS: positions 6–7, the last prepared defensive area.

Area anchors, prepared platforms and trench geometry are in
scripts/ardennes/world.gd. Firing positions face the road approach. Infantry
slots are placed relative to each emplacement's facing, with flank and rear
positions instead of an arbitrary cluster of spawn points.

COMMAND
The commander owns the current sector. Three maneuver groups each direct three
squads. A squad leader is also a combat actor. On a leader's death, a living
subordinate inherits the command role. Groups select different uncleared areas
inside the active sector where possible. When that sector is cleared, the next
sector is announced and assigned. No player command or kill is required to
activate this system.

QUICK PLAN SELECTION
scripts/ardennes/tactical_plans.json contains named reusable plans:

hold         Remain within the defended perimeter; choose cover, dig, shift
             to a reachable firing point. Threats do not cancel the boundary.
support      Assault order with cautious movement. Move to a supporting line
             and cover other elements. It is not a permanent hold order.
flank_left   Select the area's left approach, using alternating moving and
             firing elements and cover when available.
flank_right  Select the area's right approach with the same shared behavior.
clear        Close on surviving contacts in the assigned area.

scripts/ardennes/tactics.gd chooses these plans and creates formation goals.
This separates a plan's purpose from the actor's individual pathfinding.
Formation spacing, fixed area geometry and approach offsets are reused on
subsequent orders; the AI still computes a path around current obstacles.

PERCEPTION AND FIRING
Line of sight identifies a target. Gunshots provide an uncertain location;
leaders share that contact within their group. Distant shots outside an
American squad's assigned sector do not continually cancel its mission.
Nearby threats and visible enemies can still demand a response.

Holding and assault temperament are separate. A hold actor is constrained to
its perimeter even when threatened. Cautious assault actors do advance, but
use slower bounds. Cover reservations discourage squads from piling into the
same point. Moving actors maintain separation; entrenched actors may be closer.

Before firing, actors check both the center line and the dispersion cone for
allies, including a short prediction of their movement. Repeated blocked shots
prompt repositioning. Grenade selection checks current and predicted friendly
positions near the target. These checks reduce accidents, but the damage code
does not exempt teammates.

Each weapon has a real loaded count, reserve, reload interval and condition.
Each soldier begins with two grenades. Empty or damaged weapons do not create
new ammunition. Mounted gunners have finite personal-rifle backup ammunition.
A truck M2 gunner who cannot see an engagement for a while can leave the mounted
weapon and continue with the personal rifle. M2s never become handheld rifles.
Mounted weapon reserve loads represent supplies at the position or vehicle;
they are not an individual infantry backpack allowance.

AUTHORING EXCEPTIONS
The opening has two concealed Panzerfaust performers and two guaranteed vehicle
impacts. Only those projectiles are authored hits. The infantry battle, other
rockets, bullets, grenade decisions and casualties are simulated. If a truck is
lost early, the convoy can stop early and bring the end strikes forward.

VALIDATION
reviews/ardennes-tests.json records mechanics and a seeded autonomous battle.
reviews/ardennes-performance.json records a live GPU run, resolution and timing.
Screenshots and logs are in reviews/ and logs/. The tests observe AI without
having the player shoot or issue orders. They do not prove human-level tactics
or guarantee that the Americans will win any particular run.
