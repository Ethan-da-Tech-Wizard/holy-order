HOLY ORDER — ROAD OF IRON
Winter convoy mission, v0.4.0

START
Double-click click2play.desktop, then choose DEPLOY. The launcher opens the
start screen; it does not automatically begin the battle or shut down the PC.
click2play-desert.desktop opens the original First Contact mission.

THIS TEST
A fictional Ardennes encounter in winter 1944–45. Nine CCKW-style trucks carry
107 American AI soldiers and you; twelve is the chosen load per truck, not a
claim about maximum seating. Eight German camps hold 80 defenders and twelve
finite-ammunition MG positions. The map has broad hills, woodland, prepared
firing positions, timber shelters, sandbags, trenches and artillery craters.

You are still the displaced armored knight, with sword, shovel, gauntlets,
four Mk 2 grenades and a BAR: 20 loaded rounds and 120 spare rounds. No voice
acting is needed: orders and the knight's remarks appear as subtitles.

THE CONVOY
The cargo bed has physical sides, front and roof. The rear stays open, and your
seat is in the back row. Look, lean, crouch or go prone, but neither you nor the
AI passengers can fire or throw grenades while aboard. The intact trip takes
roughly a minute; damage and traffic can change both its duration and endpoint.

Roadside weapons shoot real rounds into the moving convoy. Panels can be
penetrated, passengers can die, and wheels can be damaged. One ruined wheel
slows a truck; two disable it. Following drivers attempt to pass on a clear
shoulder. Each truck checks its own progress: less than about ten feet over
six seconds orders its surviving passengers to dismount. One stopped truck
does not automatically end the entire convoy drive.

The final Panzerfaust ambush depends on actual arrivals and living launchers
with a firing line. No arriving trucks means no authored strikes; one eligible
truck receives only the front strike. A second strike needs a distinct rear
truck still in the kill zone. The two special projectiles guarantee their
selected impact once fired. Their soldiers are visible and killable; they do
not teleport or get replaced. Other fire and casualties are simulated.

THE ASSAULT
Surviving dismounted troops join balanced LEFT and RIGHT sections according to
their positions. You can follow either. Both sections first clear the ambush
hilltops, then work back along their own ridge through the roadside gun camps.
The compass and J orders page show the current targets. Nearby crossfire can
make soldiers suppress or evade without changing their assigned hilltop.
Killing the final German completes this prototype mission.

AI AND TERRAIN
Attackers alternate moving and firing, choose cover, maintain spacing, and
retain their objectives. Under heavy fire without a visible enemy, they can
crawl forward. Defenders remain in their encampments, move to local cover,
lean or change stance, and dig cover when time allows. Leaders are combatants;
living subordinates take over if they die. AI grenade throws have a windup and
recheck for nearby friendlies before release. Weapons and grenades are finite.

Hills now have broader, gentler approaches. Prone collision follows the slope,
and first-person upright boots are hidden while crawling. Shovel work can dig
deep, wide pits. Grenades make shallow soil scars while close explosions can
still open traversable holes in timber cover. Very steep player-dug pits can
still be difficult to leave; cut a wider approach with the shovel.

INVENTORY AND DESTRUCTION
One firearm at a time; swapping drops the previous gun with its loaded rounds
and condition. Broken guns are pickable but cannot fire. Corpses, detached
parts and dropped equipment remain for the mission. Loot transfers the actual
remaining stores. You can carry four grenades total; excess stays on the body.
German grenades retain their type. MG pickups use portable ammunition loads,
with excess belt ammunition left on the ground.

Spare ammunition shares a pack with 24 space units and a 7.5 kg ammunition limit.
This is a scenario belt-and-pack allowance, not a universal historical maximum.
Loaded ammunition is on the weapon, separate from spare pack space. Containers
are tracked individually; a partly full magazine still occupies its full space.

Feed                              Pack space each     Additional type limit
20-round BAR magazine             3.0                 160 spare rounds
8-round M1 en-bloc clip            1.0                 128 spare rounds
5-round Mauser stripper clip       0.5                 120 spare rounds
10-round G43 magazine              2.0                 120 spare rounds
32-round MP40 magazine             3.0                 192 spare rounds
50-round 7.92 mm MG belt            6.0                 200 spare rounds
100-round .50 belt                15.0                 also limited by weight

Space and weight limits apply across all calibers together. Different calibers
never convert into one another. Repacking compatible cartridges from another
feed takes an extra reload delay; detailed individual cartridge handling is
abstracted. Empty containers are discarded. Tab lists the actual containers.
DROP SPARE AMMO PACK places that ammunition on the ground; F recovers what fits.
Uncollected ammunition is retained on its body or dropped bundle.


PERFORMANCE AND LIMITS
Bullet marks on trucks are instanced instead of rebuilding a mesh for every
round. Accumulated wear still destroys panels; penetration and occupant damage
remain active. Terrain edits cache vertex heights; AI routes cache corners;
cover reservations, stationary collision updates, soldier meshes, tree draw
distance and shadow resolution have also been adjusted to reduce work.

The local RTX 3070 Laptop run at 1440x900 / 4x MSAA recorded a 16.7 ms median
frame (about 60 FPS), 20.9 ms at the 95th percentile and 24.1 ms at the 99th.
The 125-second rear-seat/foot-combat sample had no measured frame above 65 ms.
It excludes screenshot readbacks and does not cover every possible viewpoint.
Details: reviews/ardennes-performance.json.

This remains a procedural prototype with simplified models and animations.
Destruction is finite and budgeted: soil supports pits/trenches, not tunnels
or overhangs; there is no general structural-engineering collapse simulation.
AI can still hesitate in complex rubble, and automated tests do not establish
human-level tactics or guarantee victory. Convoy casualties and difficulty
need player testing. No multiplayer, campaign saves or controller mapping.

See NEXT-TEST.txt for the handoff, ARDENNES-TACTICS.txt for the AI description,
and reviews/ardennes-tests.json for the actual mechanics results.
