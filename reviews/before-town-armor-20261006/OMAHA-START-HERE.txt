HOLY ORDER — THE LONGEST SHORE
Mission 03 / Omaha-inspired landing / 6 June 1944

START
Double-click click2play.desktop, choose OMAHA in the shared mission menu, then Deploy.
Winter and Desert are in the same menu. Esc > Mission Menu changes missions.
Only one mission is loaded at a time; old installed shortcuts also open this menu.

THE FORCE
1,200 Americans total, including the player and naval crews; 100 German defenders.
Two waves of 600: twenty Higgins craft per wave, each carrying 27 assault passengers,
two Browning M1919 gunners, and one coxswain. The player replaces passenger 25
in the tenth first-wave craft, near the back of its nine tightly packed rows.
That is 1,080 assault troops including the player, plus 120 naval crew, with no
infinite reinforcements. Both sides carry grenades and finite ammunition.

THE LANDING
First-wave craft advance abreast across a concentrated 228-metre frontage. Wave two
circles offshore, then approaches in formation and lands 240 seconds after
wave one. First landing is 32 seconds after Deploy; second landing is 4:32.
Weapons and walking are locked while packed in the craft. Look, crouch, prone,
and lean still work. Ramps lower, the front files leave first, then your file
moves you down the ramp into shallow water. No instant F teleport out at sea.

Empty boats begin returning 24 seconds after their landing. They turn into
separate return lanes 6 metres beside the assault lanes and disappear into fog
before reaching the reserve holding area.
Their drivers and gun crews leave with them. Returned boats no longer collide,
render, attract enemy attention, or obstruct the second wave.

Hull panels accumulate independent damage. Fresh panels stop rounds; worn
panels permit reduced-energy penetration into real passenger hitboxes, then fail.
The driver has a reinforced shield and very high health. Other occupants have
normal health. Thin offshore fog conceals the formation until it crosses the
visibility line. Friendly Brownings provide covering fire.

THE BEACH
An authored 576-metre-wide playable coastal sector: wet sand and surf, rows of Czech
hedgehogs and mined posts, crater cover, a broken seawall, three barbed-wire rows,
tall graded bluffs, eight concrete bunker complexes, traversable firing slits,
rear entrances, interconnected dug trenches, a fortified 48-building village, and 8 ruined cottages along the beach road.
The visible coastline continues beyond the playable area. This is NOT a surveyed
1:1 reconstruction of all Omaha Beach. Landforms and fortification positions
are authored for this game. Soldier models and animation remain prototype art;
photographic ground/concrete textures do not make the entire game photorealistic.

The surf-to-first-wire crossing is 100 metres (previously 214). The three belts
stand at 100, 138 and 176 metres inland. Each craft has its own breach lane.
Engineers open the intact wire ahead of their file before considering other gaps;
riflemen share nearby openings within 18 metres instead of crossing the beach.
Each file uses the nearest seawall opening, normally a detour of six metres or less.
Engineers check for deployment immediately on reaching the wire; other infantry
provide brief covering bursts and can fire less accurately while advancing.

32 German mounted MG42 positions: three protected bunker positions and one
sandbag position per strongpoint, including enfilade arcs along the beach.
Eight additional infantry carry MG42s. Riflemen use Kar98k, G43 and MP40 weapons.
Americans use Garands, BARs, carbines and fourteen flamethrowers. The player starts
with a Garand, 8 loaded / 96 spare rounds, four grenades, sword, fists and shovel.
The knight starts with 300 health in all three missions; the health bar uses that maximum.

BREACHES AND OBJECTIVES
120 AI engineers carry three finite Bangalore sections each, sixty carriers per
wave. Three are spaced through each boat: front, middle and rear of the file.
A surviving engineer can breach successive belts, but carriers can still die. A carrier lays its charge at a wire segment, retreats during
the two-second fuse, then that ONE eight-metre segment opens. Adjacent segments and the
other rows remain intact. Soldiers share existing gaps; nearby allies can recover
unused charges from a fallen carrier within 24 metres. The player never receives a Bangalore.
If engineers fail, work around a wire flank, or use 16 close shovel strokes to
cut one segment while under cover. Clear at least three strongpoints and all
village guards, establish control inland, and link with the second wave to win.

AI selects the nearest detectable target with real line-of-sight checks. It has
no player priority. Quiet crouched/prone movement shortens detection distance;
approaching from behind works until close range, exposure or firing reveals you.
Knowing where a gunshot happened does not allow sight through concrete.
Suppression, reloads, ammunition exhaustion, grenades, friendly fire, looting,
equipment damage and persistent bodies remain part of the battle.

CONTROLS
WASD move, mouse look; LMB attack; RMB sights; Shift sprint.
C toggle crouch; Z toggle prone; Q/E lean; Space jump / mantle.
R reload; G timed grenade throw; F interact / loot / swap firearm.
1 sword; 2 firearm; 3 fists; 4 shovel. Tab inventory; J orders.
Shovel: hold LMB to dig deeper; add RMB for a wider cut.
Esc pause; F11 fullscreen; F6 cycles Low / Standard / High. F10 hides the FPS counter.

PERFORMANCE
Low graphics is the default: Omaha uses 40% internal 3D resolution; Winter and
Desert use 50%. No
MSAA, no screen-space effects or shadows, cheaper particles, pooled gunshot
voices and fewer detailed models. Text stays sharp. Standard is 75% resolution;
High is 100% with short-range sun shadows. The bundled engine's lighter Mobile
renderer is used; nothing was downloaded or installed for this update.

Distant soldiers use interpolated instanced meshes; Low keeps at most 16 detailed
soldiers nearby. There are 168 terrain chunks, down from 360, and 156 wire
sections (about 175,000 triangles). Buildings, tracers, flashes, persistent bodies and dropped weapons are instanced.
Offshore reserves sleep until their release; active troops use spatial target
searches. Low-mode infantry samples the actual deformable ground heights and
sweeps its posture capsule against solid cover, keeping full movement speed.
AI decisions are spread across rendered frames. Gun timers retain fractional
tick time. Movement is interpolated between simulation ticks; mouse look stays
responsive at render speed. Local soil cells refine only where actually dug.

Grenades leave small shallow soil scars and local holes in thin town walls and
other vulnerable cover. Remaining pieces keep collision. Thick concrete bunkers
and winter truck armor retain their protection. Geometry edits are queued with
a bounded work budget. WEAPON-RATES.txt documents firearm timing and its sources.

Headless checks measure CPU work, not laptop GPU frame rate. Use the on-screen
FPS counter during actual play to assess the complete result.
