HOLY ORDER / REUSABLE WINTER ENCOUNTER PLANS — v0.4.0

This describes the game's AI implementation, not a real-world field manual.

MISSION STRUCTURE
Surviving dismounted Americans are assigned to LEFT / RIGHT groups. New
arrivals are sorted by their position relative to the road and fill the less
populated group to keep the initial split balanced. Each group has four squad
slots. Casualties can subsequently unbalance the groups. The player is free
to choose a side.

LEFT  starts at camp 6, then backtracks through 4, 2, 0.
RIGHT starts at camp 7, then backtracks through 5, 3, 1.
Both initial ambush hills must be clear before either group starts backtracking.
After that, the two groups progress independently. Their route remains fixed;
they can use the road or cover on the way without swapping objectives merely
because a shot came from the opposite side.

The commander, group leaders and squad leaders are ordinary combat actors.
Living subordinates inherit a vacant role. All Germans hold their encampments;
all American foot troops have assault orders. No player input is needed to
activate the command system or keep the mission moving.

REUSABLE PLANS
scripts/ardennes/tactical_plans.json holds plan names, approach distances and
spacing values read by tactics.gd and battle.gd. Area geometry and navigation
remain in world.gd. The plan is selected before individual pathfinding.

hold         Remain inside the local defended perimeter. Find cover, entrench,
             lean, shift to a firing position, or crawl under pressure.
support      Move toward the assigned hill with alternating firing/moving turns.
flank_left   Take the left section's approach while retaining the same objective.
flank_right  Take the right section's approach with shared movement behavior.
clear        Close on the assigned camp from a shorter approach distance.

SENSING AND MOVEMENT
Sight identifies actual actors. Gunshots communicate an uncertain location;
leaders can relay contact positions, not an unseen target through terrain.
Nearby incoming rounds and hits increase threat pressure. Movement plans react
to that pressure without replacing the mission destination.

Attacking troops alternate short movement bounds with firing. Cover selection
prefers progress toward the objective. Heavily suppressed troops without a
visible opponent crawl toward the mission instead of freezing at a noise
location. Stance changes affect both the model and the collision volume.
Local steering, obstacle steps, route refreshes and separation help movement.

Defenders use shorter local movements and never deliberately chase outside
their guard perimeter. Idle defenders can dig several shovel strokes to make
wider/deeper cover. Nearby terrain changes invalidate affected cover choices
and route caches. A two-meter navigation grid is still an approximation; rubble
and narrow gaps can cause hesitation. This is not human tactical judgment.

FIRING AND INVENTORY
Friendly-fire checks include the bullet dispersion cone and a short movement
prediction. Blocked fire causes a retry delay and can prompt repositioning.
Grenade targets are checked for friendlies at selection and again after the
visible throw windup. These checks reduce accidents; teammates are not immune.
Suppression shots are limited and require a recent contact. Actual loaded
rounds, reserves, reload intervals, weapon condition and grenades are consumed.
Empty MGs switch to a separately tracked personal-rifle supply, never a refill.

CONVOY AND AUTHORING EXCEPTIONS
No passenger can fire, including the old mounted-gunner slots. Cargo side/front
curtains and roof are physical; the open rear provides a view. Trucks react to
wheel damage, spacing and local obstacles independently. A six-second movement
window detects trucks that cannot make about ten feet of progress and unloads
them. Passing is attempted only when a neighboring lane is clear.

The two reserved Panzerfaust soldiers are visible, finite-ammo and killable.
A front/rear strike requires an actual occupied arrival and a living shooter
with range and line of sight. No arrivals skips the sequence; one arrival has
no invented rear target. The rear condition is rechecked before its delayed
shot. Only those selected projectiles have authored guaranteed impacts.
Everything else, including transit casualties, runs through the battle system.

VALIDATION
reviews/ardennes-tests.json: mechanics and a four-minute autonomous simulation.
reviews/ardennes-performance.json: timed GPU rendering and its limitations.
reviews/ardennes-*.png: start screen, truck view, posture, weapon and combat views.
logs/: execution logs. Tests do not guarantee AI victory or final game balance.
